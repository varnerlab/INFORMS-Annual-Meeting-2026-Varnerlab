  \note{\footnotesize
    \vnnotehead{Rebalancing controller: the forecast}
    \vnnoterow{Drift}{$\mu_{g,i} = \alpha_i + \beta_i\, \tilde g_M$, the online single-index estimate of the expected
      growth rate of the log price, so the GBM needs no Itô correction.}
    \vnnoterow{Covariance}{$\Sigma_g = \sigma_M^2\, \beta\beta^{\top} + (1 - \lambda)\, \hat\Omega_\varepsilon +
      \lambda\, \mathrm{diag}(\hat\Omega_\varepsilon)$, with $\hat\Omega_\varepsilon$ the sample residual covariance
      over the last 126 days. λ was chosen by the QLIKE loss of 21-day variance forecasts for random portfolios on
      2014–2024, which picked $\lambda = 0$.}
    \vnnoterow{Paths}{1,000 paths over 21 trading days. Each day $\ln S_i$ advances by $g_i\, \Delta t$ with
      $g \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_g, \Sigma_g)$, independent across days and paths, starting from the decision day's
      closes.}
    \vnnoterow{Wealth}{Target shares times the simulated prices, plus the leftover cash growing at the 3-month
      T-bill rate. The forecast leaves out trading costs, the overnight gap before the fill and borrow fees.}
    \vnnoterow{Band}{Mean $\pm$ 1.96 SD of the simulated wealth, for each day 1 to 20 after the re-plan.}
    \vnnoterow{Seeds}{Forecast paths are seeded by the run seed and the decision date, so a run reproduces exactly.}
    \vnnoterow{Same model}{The allocation uses the same $\mu_g$ and $\Sigma_g$ in its volatility cap and benchmarks,
      so each book is forecast with the model that sized it.}}
\end{frame}
\note{\footnotesize
  \vnnotehead{Rebalancing controller: the triggers}
  \vnnoterow{Order of checks}{At each close, the first that holds: cash revisit (the last plan was all cash and 21
    days have passed), drawdown (wealth more than 8\% below its peak since the last drawdown stop), horizon (21 trading days), band
    exit, then a basket change at a timer pick.}
  \vnnoterow{Drawdown reset}{A drawdown re-plan resets the peak to the current wealth, so the stop fires once per
    fresh 8\% loss (88.5\% of decisions were drawdown re-plans without it).}
  \vnnoterow{A re-plan}{Solves the same allocation from the shares held, with that day's estimates. No trigger
    changes the objective or the limits, so a drawdown stop does not cut exposure by itself.}
  \vnnoterow{Basket picks}{On the first day, every 21 trading days and at every band exit. A timer pick that changes
    the names also forces a re-plan.}
  \vnnoterow{Re-plans per run}{27 in the run shown: 17 at the horizon, 7 band exits, 3 drawdown stops.}
  \vnnoterow{Related work}{Multi-period convex trading (Boyd et al., 2017) plans a sequence of trades and executes
    the first each period. This controller solves one period and trades only when a trigger fires; the band is a
    no-trade region on the portfolio value rather than on the weights.}}

\note{\textbf{If asked whether the triggers beat a fixed schedule:}\par\medskip
  We reran everything on a fixed 21-day schedule. Ending wealth is not consistently higher with the triggers: on
  real prices they raised it in 38 of 70 combinations with the hedge off and 26 of 70 with it on, and some
  combinations moved by close to 0.2. The triggers take about a point off the worst drawdown, on real prices and
  in simulated sell-offs. In the sell-offs the margin over SPY holds without the triggers, and part of it belongs
  to the simulated market itself: the equal-weight universe also beat SPY there.\par\bigskip
  \textbf{If asked whether the forecast's drift and covariance are updated:}\par\medskip
  At every re-plan. Each one rebuilds $\mu_g$ and $\Sigma_g$ from that day's α, β, $\tilde g_M$ and $\sigma_M$ and
  the last 126 days of residuals, and holds them constant over the 21 forecast days. Between re-plans the estimates
  keep updating after every close, but the band is not recomputed: wealth is checked against the band stored at the
  last re-plan until a trigger fires.}
